While path durations vary non-monotonically—ranging from \(0.734\:\mathrm{s}\) (\(p_{\rm safe}=0.95\)) to \(1.029\:\mathrm{s}\) (\(p_{\rm safe}=0.9\))—the planner does not exhibit a strict trade-off between safety and efficiency. These fluctuations may stem from RRT’s inherent stochasticity, but the consistent success rate confirms robustness in probabilistic collision checking and belief propagation, even with tighter safety margins.

\section{Discussion}
Our implementation of Chance-Constrained RRT demonstrates that incorporating probabilistic safety directly into the planning process is both practical and effective in moderately cluttered environments. Across all tested safety thresholds, the planner consistently found valid paths within the allotted time, with a 100\% success rate in the static scenario. This result validates the integration of Gaussian uncertainty propagation~\cite{blackmore2006probabilistic} and probabilistic collision checking~\cite{luders2010chance} into the RRT expansion process.

As expected, increasing the safety threshold \(p_{\text{safe}}\) led to longer paths and greater clearance from obstacles, as seen in both the quantitative results and the trajectory plots in Fig.~\ref{fig:psafe_levels}. \section{Conclusion}
This work presents a functional and extensible implementation of Chance-Constrained RRT within the OMPL framework, enabling real-time motion planning under uncertainty with tunable safety guarantees. Our planner augments RRT~\cite{lavalle1998rapidly} with Gaussian belief propagation and efficient probabilistic collision checks~\cite{blackmore2006probabilistic}, yielding safe trajectories without incurring the heavy computational costs of deterministic robust planning methods.We have made a meaningful contribution to the OMPL library and created modular tools that enable experimentation with probabilistic safety-aware planning. Our results show that the algorithm is well-suited for settings where the environment is partially observable or odometry is noisy. 

Experimental results in cluttered 2D environments show that CC-RRT achieves a favorable trade-off between path efficiency and safety, maintaining real-time performance even as safety thresholds increase. Our findings replicate and extend the behaviors reported in foundational CC-RRT literature~\cite{luders2010chance}, offering practical evidence that chance-constrained methods are viable for real-world robotic systems.\section{Conclusion}
